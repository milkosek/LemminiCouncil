# Vision

## The idea

**Lemmini Council** is a personal project and a personal mission. It is a place that stops,
deliberates and is willing to go against the current, even when that is the harder path.

The theme is **ethics in IT and in business**, with some philosophy mixed in. It is written from the
point of view of a software engineer who ships real products and has to make these calls at work.

## Goals, in priority order

1. **A blog first.** The Council speaks through writing. Posts are the core of the site and the first
   thing that ships.
2. **Room to grow.** Lemmini Council is the umbrella for my personal projects. Any project that grows
   big enough can get its own subdomain and its own identity, linked back to the Council.
3. **A personal brand as a software engineer.** A portfolio and about page, with links to GitHub and
   LinkedIn. It matters, but it comes after the writing.

## Editorial direction

- **Practical over preachy.** Concrete cases (a dark pattern taken apart, a decision from a real
  sprint, a policy that looked fine on paper) over abstract lectures. Philosophy is seasoning, not the
  main course.
- **Honest about trade-offs.** Ethical questions in business rarely have free answers. Name the cost of
  doing the right thing.
- **For people who build things:** engineers, product people, founders and anyone who has been asked
  to "just ship it".

## The site is the argument

The site should practise what it preaches. That makes it a portfolio piece in its own right:

- No third-party trackers and no ad tech. Analytics, if any, are cookie-less and aggregate.
- No dark patterns: no nag modals, no fake urgency, no consent walls.
- Third-party embeds (video, audio) load only when the visitor asks for them.
- Accessible (WCAG AA as a floor), fast, and readable without JavaScript.
- Open source, so anyone can check that the claims above are true.

## Out of scope for now

- User accounts and logins
- A server or backend of any kind (the first release is fully static)
- Monetisation
